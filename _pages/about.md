Hi, I'm Aditya, a PhD candidate in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at Virginia Tech, advised by Prof. Mark Paul in the [Nonlinear Dynamics and Pattern Formation](https://paul.me.vt.edu) group. I study large chaotic systems, from lattices of coupled maps to convecting fluids. My work looks at how they organize themselves in space and time, and how the way their parts interact shapes the chaos they produce.

Research
======
Many systems in nature, such as convecting and turbulent fluids, contain an enormous number of interacting degrees of freedom spread out in space. Their dynamics are often chaotic in both space and time, which is called *spatiotemporal chaos*. I use computation and dynamical systems theory, especially Lyapunov exponents and covariant Lyapunov vectors (CLVs), to build a physical picture of this chaos. I ask which directions of instability drive the dynamics, what spatial structures they carry, and how both depend on the way the system is coupled in space.

My broader interests include chaos, nonlinear dynamics, pattern formation, fluid dynamics, and complex systems.

**Spatial coupling in spatiotemporal chaos.** Using large lattices of coupled maps as a model system, I study how the strength and range of spatial coupling change chaotic dynamics.
- **Diffusive coupling** ([*Chaos*, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0210661)): the Lyapunov spectrum and fractal dimension can be described analytically using the eigenvalues of the coupling operator. The leading CLV is highly localized, and it becomes less localized as the coupling gets stronger.
- **Coupling of increasing range** ([*IJBC*, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01424)): I developed a theory that predicts the Lyapunov spectrum and the spatial structure of the CLVs from the coupling matrix alone, independent of the details of the map. The CLVs split into two regimes, and the boundary between them sits at roughly the fractal dimension of the dynamics.

**Mean flow and spiral defect chaos in convection.** Rayleigh–Bénard convection, a fluid layer heated from below, can form *spiral defect chaos*, a disordered state of rolls and spirals that is constantly rearranging. I model it with the generalized Swift–Hohenberg equation coupled to a mean flow. For this work I built pseudospectral, exponential time-differencing solvers for both the pattern and its tangent-space dynamics, and I run them on Virginia Tech's HPC clusters. I am investigating how the strength of the mean flow changes the tangent-space dynamics of spiral defect chaos. I am also testing extensivity, which asks whether the fractal dimension of the chaotic attractor grows in proportion to the area of the system.
